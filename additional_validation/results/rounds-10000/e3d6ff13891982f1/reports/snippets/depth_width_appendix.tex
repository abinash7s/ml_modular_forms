\section{Additional validation}
\label{app:additional-validation}

This study examines width, depth and optimizer dependence on two representative power families. The planned depth/width study comprises $78$ seeded neural fits. This report contains $78$ distinct completed fits. Uniform networks have one, three or six hidden ReLU layers; the original Net1 and Net3 are included as references. The wider and deeper architectures are compared on the same data splits and training budget within each study.

All test metrics are evaluated after predictions have been returned to the signed physical weight $w$:
\begin{align}
 \mathrm{MAPE} &= \frac{100}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n
              \frac{|\widehat w_i-w_i|}{|w_i|},\\
 \mathrm{RMSE} &= \sqrt{\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(\widehat w_i-w_i)^2}.
\end{align}
The CHL datasets have nonzero weights. Thus their MAPE requires no omission of
zero targets and is not a percentage error of a logarithm. When a logarithmic
target is fitted, its transformation is inverted before these metrics are
computed, including the known sign and family-dependent conversion from power
to weight. Training and validation losses remain in the fitted target space;
their numerical values should not be compared across different target transformations.
Recorded input transformations: \texttt{L2}, \texttt{LogAbsL2}. Recorded target transformations:
\texttt{LogAbsPower}, \texttt{StandardizedWeight}. Recorded training budgets: 10000 rounds.
For \texttt{LogAbsL2}, coefficient magnitudes are logged and the resulting vector is normalized to unit Euclidean norm; exactly zero coefficients are mapped to zero while retaining their positions.
For \texttt{L2}, raw coefficient vectors are normalized to unit Euclidean norm.
Standardized weights use only the training-set mean and sample standard deviation; the same transformation is applied to validation and test data.
For \texttt{LogAbsPower}, the fitted target is $\log|s|$, where $s$ is the power; a predicted value $z$ is decoded as $\widehat w=\operatorname{sgn}(s)K_0 e^z$, with the known weight $K_0$ of the parent form.
The final checkpoint and the checkpoint minimizing validation loss are reported
separately. Test errors play no part in checkpoint selection. Error bars are sample
standard deviations over training seeds on fixed data partitions, not confidence
intervals over alternative partitions or modular-form families.

\paragraph{Positive CHL powers, $N=3$.}

Among completed three-seed configurations, the lowest mean validation-selected test MAPE is obtained by Net3 with ADAM: $0.400\pm0.0987$\%.

Among completed three-seed configurations, the lowest mean validation-selected test RMSE is obtained by Net3 with ADAM: $12.8\pm2.35$.

The same configuration minimizes both reported mean test metrics within this grid.

At width $16$ with RMSProp, changing from one to three hidden layers changes mean test MAPE from $5.06\%$ to $0.851\%$, and mean test RMSE from $148$ to $34.0$.

At width $128$ with RMSProp, changing from one to three hidden layers changes mean test MAPE from $0.699\%$ to $0.765\%$, and mean test RMSE from $31.2$ to $33.5$.

For $1\times 128$, Adam and RMSProp respectively give mean test MAPE $3.73\%$ and $0.699\%$, and mean test RMSE $113$ and $31.2$. This comparison concerns the recorded optimizer settings, not independently tuned optimizer families.

For Net3, Adam and RMSProp respectively give mean test MAPE $0.400\%$ and $0.998\%$, and mean test RMSE $12.8$ and $40.3$. This comparison concerns the recorded optimizer settings, not independently tuned optimizer families.

Comparisons of different depths also change parameter count; they do not isolate depth at fixed capacity. Test rankings are descriptive and are not used to choose checkpoints or tune hyperparameters.

\paragraph{Negative eta powers.}

Among completed three-seed configurations, the lowest mean validation-selected test MAPE is obtained by $6\times 128$ with ADAM: $0.0509\pm0.00801$\%.

Among completed three-seed configurations, the lowest mean validation-selected test RMSE is obtained by $6\times 128$ with ADAM: $0.0345\pm0.00147$.

The same configuration minimizes both reported mean test metrics within this grid.

At width $16$ with ADAM, changing from one to three hidden layers changes mean test MAPE from $1.29\%$ to $0.316\%$, and mean test RMSE from $0.725$ to $0.260$.

At width $128$ with ADAM, changing from one to three hidden layers changes mean test MAPE from $0.153\%$ to $0.0510\%$, and mean test RMSE from $0.0698$ to $0.0392$.

For $1\times 128$, Adam and RMSProp respectively give mean test MAPE $0.153\%$ and $0.185\%$, and mean test RMSE $0.0698$ and $0.160$. This comparison concerns the recorded optimizer settings, not independently tuned optimizer families.

For Net1, Adam and RMSProp respectively give mean test MAPE $42.6\%$ and $0.147\%$, and mean test RMSE $20.1$ and $0.123$. This comparison concerns the recorded optimizer settings, not independently tuned optimizer families.

Comparisons of different depths also change parameter count; they do not isolate depth at fixed capacity. Test rankings are descriptive and are not used to choose checkpoints or tune hyperparameters.

\paragraph{Training budget and checkpoint choice.}

Both checkpoint conventions are tabulated. The largest ratio of final to validation-selected mean test RMSE among complete configurations is $10.1$, for Positive CHL powers, $N=3$, Net3, ADAM ($129$ versus $12.8$). Validation selection does not guarantee lower error on every held-out test set.

\paragraph{Capacity and interpolation.}

At the final checkpoint, $2$ of $78$ completed fits have training NRMSE at most $10^{-3}$, with NRMSE normalized by the training-set standard deviation of the physical weight. This threshold is a numerical convention for near interpolation. A double-descent claim would additionally require a reproducible test-error peak near a resolved interpolation transition and a subsequent decline. Neither having more parameters than examples nor an isolated non-monotone error curve establishes that claim.

\paragraph{Scope.}
These experiments measure prediction within restricted power families. They do not establish extrapolation to new modular-form families or the necessity of neural networks. For full eta coefficient vectors, the weight is recovered exactly from $-a_1/(2a_0)$. For CHL eta products at $N>1$, the corresponding predictor is $-a_1/a_0$; at $N=1$ it is $-a_1/(2a_0)$. These identities are elementary baselines, not learned results, and apply when the required coefficients are retained.
